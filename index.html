Planning Poker

A simple, collaborative technique for agile estimation.

What is Planning Poker?

Planning Poker is a consensus-based estimation technique used by agile teams. Team members estimate the effort of a work item, such as a user story, by privately choosing a card and then revealing their choices at the same time.

How it works

  1. The product owner or facilitator describes the work item and answers questions.
  2. Each team member privately selects a card representing their estimate.
  3. Everyone reveals their card at the same time.
  4. If the estimates differ, the highest and lowest estimators explain their reasoning.
  5. The team discusses and votes again until the estimates converge.

The cards

Decks commonly use a modified Fibonacci sequence, such as 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40 and 100. The growing gaps reflect the increasing uncertainty of larger items. Many decks also include a question mark for "I need more information" and a coffee cup for "I need a break".

Why teams use it

  • Avoids anchoring: voting at the same time stops early opinions from swaying others.
  • Encourages discussion: differing estimates surface hidden assumptions and risks.
  • Includes everyone: every team member has an equal voice.
  • Builds shared understanding: the team agrees on what the work involves.

Tips for good sessions

  • Estimate relative size and complexity rather than hours.
  • Keep discussions focused and time-boxed.
  • Split items that are too large to estimate with confidence.
  • Treat estimates as a planning aid, not a commitment.
